- Phase 9, slice 1a — the notifications platform: **one place that sends messages** (`NotificationSender::send(templateCode, recipient, variables, key, related)`). A message is recorded in `notification_logs` (the queue, the retry book and the record in one; the recipient only masked, the words never kept, the address and values held ENCRYPTED until sent and then erased) and delivered by a queued job. The same key for the same message always gives the same record, so an event announced twice sends once. A failed try waits longer each time (1, 5, 30, 120, 360 minutes), then the message is **dead**; staff can see it, and send it again with a reason, until the kept address is erased after the retention period (14 days, a setting). An hourly check writes `notifications.alert` to the log when anything is dead, failing or overdue. Delivery is claimed by one conditional update, so any number of workers send it once (proved with 8 simultaneous processes). **Words** are staff-editable per language with `{{ variable }}` placeholders only — no logic, nothing evaluated, values stripped of control characters and cut at 300 characters, a value never read as a template, an email subject always one line, plain text that the layout escapes; each save is a new version (append-only history); a variable the message does not declare, or stray braces, is refused. **Marketing** needs the person's yes on that channel and waits out their quiet hours (Dhaka time, including hours that cross midnight); messages about someone's own order always go. Login codes (OTP) stay direct: immediate and never stored. Staff screens (permissions `notifications.view` / `notifications.manage`): the messages with a preview using example values, version history, switching a message off, and the delivery list with filters and "send again". Modules declare their messages with `TemplateRegistry`; moving the existing order, booking and download messages onto the platform is slice 1b. Tests: 25 platform + 11 screen feature tests + 1 multi-process test; 27 rules each broken once to prove tests fail.
- Phase 9, slice 1b — the existing messages move onto the platform: the **order confirmation** (text and email; cash-on-delivery gets its own wording), **shipping and delivery** news, **cancelled and refunded** news, the **download new-version** email and every **booking** message (confirmed, reminders, moved, ended, review question) are now declared as templates (staff can edit their words per language) and sent with `NotificationSender`. Each message has a fixed key (order, event, owner or booking time), so a repeated event sends once; a booking moved to a new time is told again for the new time. The values are made in the language the order was placed in. A problem recording an order or booking message is thrown so the outbox tries again; for news that must never undo what it announces (cancelled, refunded, delivered) it is only logged. **Changes you will notice:** the order email is now plain text with a list of items (no longer a styled page); delivery is **asynchronous**, so production needs the queue worker and the scheduler running (the platform's own retries replace the old booking retry loop). Removed: `CustomerSms`, `OrderPlacedMail`, `NewVersionMail`, `BookingMail` and their views and word files. Tests: checkout, courier events, downloads and booking tests rewritten for the new path (not weakened) plus new ones for per-order keys, no email without an address, the order's language, and 13 wiring rules broken once each (12 caught; the 13th, a reminder/review key without the kind, is equivalent because the template code is part of the key).
- Phase 9, slice 2 — preferences, consent and privacy tools. **Messages & preferences** (`/account/preferences`): marketing yes/no per channel (email, text message) and quiet hours; every CHANGE of an answer is also written to the append-only `notification_consent_events` (what, when, from where); the profile page no longer has its own marketing checkbox (one place only; the old `customers.marketing_consent` columns are unused). **Privacy & your data** (`/account/privacy`, new `Privacy` module): a customer can ask for a copy of their data (built in the background from every module's own part, only their rows, encrypted at rest, one a day, kept 7 days, downloaded after confirming it is them, each download recorded) and ask for their account to be erased. Erasure needs staff approval (`/admin/privacy`, permissions `privacy.view` / `privacy.manage`; rejecting needs a reason that the customer is told), is refused while an order is still being handled or a booking is still to come, and removes the sign-in (name, phone, password, email made unusable, sessions and tokens ended), profile, addresses, cart, message choices and unsent messages, and clears device details from download history. **Orders, payments, refunds and tax lines are kept** with the contact and delivery address replaced by placeholders; the database allows this only as exact placeholders, once (new trigger on `orders`; `download_logs` device details may only be cleared). Each part is safe to repeat, so a half-finished erasure continues where it stopped. The customer is told by email and text on the contact they had. New message templates (staff may change the words): data copy ready, erased, not approved. Daily clean-up deletes expired copies. Tests: 14 preferences + 48 privacy feature tests (ownership, replay, duplicates, resume, database rules).
- Phase 9, slice 3 — account gaps. **Buy again** (button on `/account/orders/{order}`, own orders only): every line goes into the cart at today's price under the cart's own rules; a line that cannot go in (sold out, no longer sold, a service that needs a booking, over the limit) is skipped and listed with the reason, and where only part of a quantity is available the part that fits is added (counting what is already in the cart and the steps an item is sold in); nothing is bought and no stock is held. New Cart Contract `CartFiller`. **Wishlist** (new `Engagement` module; `/account/wishlist`, a "Save for later" button on product pages; signed-in customers only, up to 200 items): shows today's price and stock, "Move to cart" (the smallest order the item allows) and "Remove". **Back-in-stock alerts**: "Tell me when it is back" on an out-of-stock item; every five minutes the shop tells the people waiting for items that are back and still on sale, oldest first and at most twice as many people as there are units, one message each (email if we have one, otherwise a text message); an alert is told once and can be asked for again; the words are staff-editable templates. Wishlist and alerts are part of the privacy data copy and are deleted on erasure. API endpoints stay deferred like the other modules. Tests: reorder, wishlist, alerts and privacy feature tests.
- Phase 9, slice 4 — reviews and ratings. **Verified purchase only**: a customer can review a line of their own order once it is completed and paid (or a digital line once paid), not if it was cancelled or fully refunded, and up to 180 days after buying (a setting); a "Write a review" button on the order page; one review per line. A review is 1–5 stars, an optional headline and 10–2,000 characters; links and the same text twice in a month are refused; at most 5 an hour. **Moderation first**: nothing shows until staff with `reviews.moderate` approve it in `/admin/reviews`; rejecting or taking a published review down needs a reason that the author sees on `/account/reviews`, where they can also withdraw. **Product page** now shows the average, the number of reviews, a bar per star count and the published reviews ten at a time, each in the language it was written in, with the author's first name and last initial and a "Verified purchase" note. The star counts (`product_rating_stats`) change in the same transaction as each decision, so they always equal the reviews. A customer's reviews are part of their data copy and are deleted on erasure (published ones stop counting). New Orders Contract `PurchasedLines`; Catalog's variant details now carry the product id. Not built: helpful votes, abuse reports, photos, Q&A, ratings on product cards. Tests: eligibility, writing, spam rules, moderation, star-count consistency, product page, privacy.
- Phase 9, slice 5 — return and cancellation requests (customer side). **Returns portal**: a "Send items back" section on the order page (when something delivered can still be returned, within the return window, 7 days from delivery, a setting) leads to a form: lines and quantities, a reason from the five existing codes, an optional note. It uses the same `RequestReturn` action staff use, so the rules and locking are unchanged; lines are numbered on the page, no internal ids. **My returns** (`/account/returns`) shows each return with a status timeline, the shop's note and who pays for sending it back; the customer can withdraw it until the goods are received. The customer is told by text (and email) when a return is allowed or refused. **Ask us to cancel**: for a paid or confirmed order that has not been sent, a request (one open per order); staff answer in `/admin/orders/cancellation-requests` (`orders.cancel`), with a banner on the order; approving records the agreement and tells the customer, and changes no order and moves no money (a person refunds and cancels as before); refusing needs a reason the customer is told. The customer's typed words are cleared on erasure (database exception: `return_requests.reason` may only be cleared); an open return blocks erasure. Not built: free-form refund requests (support tickets, next), photos on returns. Tests: portal, withdrawal, messages, cancellation requests, privacy.
- Phase 9, slice 6 — support, canned replies and a help centre. **Tickets** (`/account/support`, signed-in customers): a subject, a kind of question, an optional order of their own, a message of up to 5,000 characters and up to 3 files (JPEG, PNG, WebP or PDF, 5 MB each); the customer follows the conversation, replies, and closes it; a closed ticket opens again on a reply for 14 days (a setting); up to 5 open at once and 5 new a day. Files are checked by what they really are, pictures are re-encoded (dropping camera and location data), the virus-scan hook is asked first, and files are kept privately and opened only by the customer or staff. **Support desk** (`/admin/support`): a queue by urgency with filters (status, who has it, kind, late, reference) and a ticket page with the conversation, the order, notes for the team only (never shown to the customer), answers that tell the customer by email or text (reference and link only), taking a ticket for yourself, and (with `support.manage`) giving it to someone, closing and reopening. A first answer is due within 24 hours by default (set under Contact details); late tickets are marked and an hourly check writes one error line to the log. **Canned replies** in both languages, plain text, filling in the customer's name and order. **Help centre** (`/help`, in the CMS): questions and answers in both languages with a search box, shown only when published (publishing needs `cms.publish`). **Contact page** (`/contact`) for everyone, from details staff enter. Customers' conversations are part of their data copy (without team notes); an open ticket blocks erasure and erasure deletes tickets, messages and files. Not built: guest tickets, WhatsApp/Messenger, live chat, reply by email, automatic assignment. Tests: tickets, files, desk, canned replies, contact details, FAQ, privacy.
- Phase 9, slice 7 — reminders about a forgotten cart (opt-in marketing, **off until `CART_RECOVERY_ENABLED=true`**). Every 15 minutes a job looks at signed-in customers' carts that have lines and whose newest line changed between 2 hours and 3 days ago (an emptied cart, for example after checkout, and guest carts are never listed), and reminds a customer **only if they agreed to that channel** (email first, otherwise text; people who said no leave no log row and no claim), through the one sender, so quiet hours (Dhaka time) hold the message until they end and the customer's account language is used. At most **one reminder per cart episode** (`cart_recovery_messages`, UNIQUE per customer and the time the newest line changed, inserted in the same transaction as the message so two workers cannot both send and a message that could not be recorded claims nothing), **at least 7 days between reminders** to the same customer and **at most 200 per run** (all in `config/engagement.php`, `cart_recovery`). The words (English and Bangla, email and text) carry no prices and always include a link to the cart and to `/account/preferences` to stop. New Contracts: Cart `AbandonedCarts`, Notifications `MarketingConsent`, IdentityAccess `AccountLocales`. The reminder rows are part of the data copy and are deleted on erasure. No discount code is sent.

Four more metrics, each defined once: units sold, units returned, return rate, sales after discounts (gross − discounts). Aging report (`CodAging`): cash not yet collected (confirmed/processing/completed COD orders) and cash collected but not yet remitted, in 0-3 / 4-7 / 8-14 / 15+ day buckets. Migrations add `cod_collections.payment_id` and a unique marker so an order has one hand-recorded collection (each shipment, later, collects its own). Tests: 23 feature tests and 5 real multi-process tests (8 simultaneous recordings of one delivery → one; 6 statements claiming one parcel → one; 5 entries of one statement → one); each database guard was removed once to prove a test fails without it. Permission `payments.cod` is enforced at the routes in slice 8.
- Slice 5 — refunds (Payments, small change in Orders): staff **request** a refund (amount, reason, optionally for one specific attempt), a person **approves** it (a different person above ৳5,000, editable setting `payments.refund_second_approver_minor`) or **rejects** it (reason required), then it is **sent** to the gateway or, when the gateway cannot do it, **completed by hand with proof** (bank/bKash reference). Money given back can never pass money received: the amount is reserved on the payment with one conditional `UPDATE … WHERE refunded_minor + ? <= captured_minor` under the payment row lock (database CHECK as backstop), and a rejection or gateway refusal gives it back. A refund sent to the gateway carries our own unique `refund_ref`; "accepted/processing" stays `processing` until the gateway says refunded (scheduled `PollRefunds` every 5 minutes; unreachable gateway → same call repeated after 60 seconds; stuck over 3 days → `refund_stuck` exception). A second paid attempt on one payment (duplicate payment) can be refunded within that attempt's own amount. `payments.refund_succeeded` reaches the order through the outbox: full → `refunded`, partial → `partially_refunded`, extra/late → note only; Bangla/English SMS; a fully refunded order can now be cancelled. Migration adds `refunds.payment_transaction_id`. Tests: 38 feature tests and 7 real multi-process tests (12 simultaneous 10% requests → exactly 10 accepted; 6 × 60% → exactly 1; four simultaneous approvals → one; six simultaneous sends → the gateway asked once); each safeguard was removed once to prove a test fails without it.
- Slice 4 — the hostile-callback suite (tests and a probe command; no behaviour change except that nothing was found wrong): a 24-story **replay suite** (duplicates, the same payment through every channel at once, browser-before-notification and the reverse, failure-then-success in one session, cancel-then-success, late "failed" notices after payment, success after the time limit, after staff cancelled the order and four days after a failure, forged notifications, amounts too small / too large / one paisa out, another currency, another transaction's answer, no bank transaction id, a notification for a transaction that is not ours, gateway down then back, two attempts both paid) in which the same money rules are checked after EVERY step: never more captured than asked, no money without a verified attempt that has its bank id, paid exactly when the order is paid, at most one "paid" announcement per payment, a paid order is never also told it expired. **Real multi-process tests** (eight OS processes, own database connections, released together): eight simultaneous verifications → one payment taken and one announcement; two attempts both reported paid → money taken once, the extra flagged for refund; one notification delivered eight times at once → stored once, paid once; "failed" reports racing "paid" ones → always paid, never undone; success racing the expiry job → either paid, or expired AND recorded as a late payment (never lost, never invented); a committed-but-never-dispatched announcement (a crash) → delivered once by four racing workers and again harmlessly on redelivery. Each safeguard was removed in turn (row lock, notification de-duplication, amount and currency checks, "can this order still take the money") and the tests failed every time.
- Slice 3 — the SSLCommerz adapter (sandbox first), written only from the saved official documentation: starts a session (`POST /gwprocess/v4/api.php`) and sends the customer to the returned `GatewayPageURL` **only if it is an https address on sslcommerz.com** (a tampered answer cannot redirect a customer elsewhere); asks about a payment by validation id (Order Validation API) or by our own reference (Transaction Query API); requests refunds and reads refund status. A validation id that turns out to belong to another transaction is dropped and our own reference is asked instead (so borrowing someone else's paid id neither pays nor falsely alarms). Amounts are read exactly in paisa; when the two BDT amount fields disagree, or the text is not an exact amount, no amount is returned and the payment is held for a person. A high-risk rating still pays but holds the order for review instead of confirming it. `verify_sign` is not used (its recipe is not documented). Partial refunds are not declared (not shown in the documentation). The store password sits in the query string of the GET calls by SSLCommerz's own design, so an HTTP error is never logged or passed on as is: only the kind of failure is, and a test checks that the password and store id appear in no table row, outbox message, note or log line. **Fixed a flaw found while reading the docs:** SSLCommerz can report a failed try and then a success under the same transaction id (the query API returns several results per id), but an attempt closed as failed could never become paid, so a real payment would have been lost. A failed, cancelled or expired attempt may now still be found paid (the database allows exactly that one move; a paid attempt never changes), for a week after it started; more than one successful payment under one reference raises a "duplicate payment" for finance. The gateway is available only when credentials are set; the live bank needs production, sandbox off and `PAYMENTS_ALLOW_LIVE=true` all together, so a development machine holding live credentials cannot reach it. Tests now blank provider credentials and fail on any real HTTP request. Opt-in sandbox checks (not in CI) are in `app/Modules/Payments/tests/Sandbox/`.
- Slice 2 — paying an order online (Payments, Orders): "Pay online" appears at checkout only when the owner has switched it on, a gateway is set up, the payments freeze is off, and the order is inside the method's amount limits; downloads and services can now be bought. The order is placed unpaid with its stock held, then the customer is sent to the gateway. **A payment becomes "paid" in exactly one place (`VerifyPayment`)**: it asks the gateway server to server about OUR reference and requires the same reference, amount and currency (and a bank transaction id, without which a refund would be impossible) under the payment's row lock; anything else is held for a person as "needs review" and never paid; repeating it, or two at once, changes nothing. The browser return page and the gateway notification only say WHICH transaction to ask about: a forged "success" page or a forged "VALID" notification leaves the order unpaid (tested). Notifications are stored exactly as received (same one twice = stored once), answered at once and processed from a queue with back-off; return links are signed, expiring and exempt from CSRF only because they are. Retry after a failure is a new numbered attempt on the same payment (`VM-2610-000123-2`); pressing "pay" twice within two minutes gives the same gateway page rather than a second session; the database allows one open payment per order. Orders follow their payment only through outbox events (paid → paid and confirmed unless flagged; failed; expired → cancelled with stock released; mismatch → flagged with a note), each applied once. Unpaid online payments expire after 30 minutes (asking the gateway one last time first, so a customer who paid is never expired); money that arrives after expiry or for a cancelled order is recorded as a late payment for staff, never lost and never silently accepted. Cash orders nobody confirms are auto-cancelled after 3 days (setting `orders.pending_cancel_days`, 0 = never), freeing stock and telling the customer in their language. Order page shows paid / due / cancelled / refunded states with "Pay now" and "Check again". Online orders are not subject to the cash-order limits. Not yet: the real SSLCommerz adapter (slice 3); refunds (slice 5); admin screens (slice 8).
- Slice 1 — payment domain and gateway contract (new `Payments` module): tables `payment_methods` (+ translations), `payments`, `payment_transactions`, `payment_webhook_events`, `refunds`, `cod_collections`, `cod_remittances` (+ items), `reconciliation_exceptions`. The database refuses: amounts of zero or less, more received than asked, more refunded than received, rewriting what was agreed (amount, order, provider), deleting any payment record, the same bank transaction or notification stored twice, and any status change the state machine does not allow (a paid payment going back to pending, a final attempt changing again); a test runs every pair of statuses through the real triggers and compares with the enums so they cannot drift apart. `PaymentGateway` contract (initiate, parseCallback, verify, status, refund, refundStatus, capabilities) with `GatewayRegistry`, and a `FakeGateway` that plays any provider answer for tests and never touches a network. Payment methods seeded once: cash on delivery (on) and "Pay online" via SSLCommerz (off until credentials are in). SSLCommerz's official documentation is saved in `docs/vendor/sslcommerz/` with what it does not say (the `verify_sign` recipe is unconfirmed, so authenticity rests on server-to-server validation).
